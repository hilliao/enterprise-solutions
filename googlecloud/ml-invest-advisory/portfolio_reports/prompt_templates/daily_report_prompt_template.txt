[PORTFOLIO HOLDINGS DATA TO ANALYZE]
The portfolio holding data is a JSON dictionary.
Its keys are ticker symbols, and its values are dictionaries with these properties:
* "Name": Name of the company, or of the ETF followed by what it holds in parentheses,
  e.g. "iShares MSCI Taiwan ETF (Taiwanese stocks)"
  (missing for some tickers; see accuracy rule 5).
* "Current Value": Real-time valuation of the holding in US dollars.
* "Previous Value": Valuation of the holding at the prior market close in US dollars.
* "shares": Number of shares held.
* "single share change in percentage": Price change percentage of a single share in 1 trading day.
* "weight in percentage": Weight of this holding in the overall portfolio.
* "__CASH" (special key): Uninvested cash.
  Its "shares" is the cash amount in US dollars, and it also has "weight in percentage".
* "__SUM" (special key): Aggregate portfolio performance,
  with "Current Value", "Previous Value", and "daily performance change in percentage".

Do NOT print, copy, or replicate this raw JSON block in your report.

[ACCURACY RULES]
Follow these rules in every section of the report:

1. Copy numbers exactly from the JSON data or the market commentary.
   Do not invent prices, price targets, earnings dates, valuation ratios, returns,
   or any other figure that is not in this prompt.
   The JSON data has no share prices, so do not calculate share prices from it.

2. A holding's daily move is its "single share change in percentage",
   and its size is its "weight in percentage".
   Never attach one holding's numbers to another holding.
   If you state the combined weight of several holdings,
   add up their "weight in percentage" and double-check the sum.

3. If you state a dollar change,
   compute it as "Current Value" minus "Previous Value" of that same entry,
   and double-check the subtraction.

4. No purchase prices or cost basis are provided.
   Do not mention gains or losses since purchase, "locking in gains", or "taking profits".

5. Identify each holding by its ticker symbol,
   and use its "Name" to say what it is or what the ETF holds.
   Some "Name" values are only the legal name of a fund trust
   (for example "iSHARES TRUST" or "SPDR SERIES TRUST") that issues many different ETFs.
   For those, and for tickers without a "Name", describe the holding only if you are sure what it is;
   otherwise say that you are unsure.

6. Only call a holding a hedge, buffer, or cushion for today if its daily change was zero or positive.
   A holding that fell less than the portfolio's "daily performance change in percentage"
   held up better than the portfolio, but it still added to the loss, so do not call it a cushion.

7. Clearly separate facts from the data, from the market commentary,
   and your own opinion or general knowledge.
